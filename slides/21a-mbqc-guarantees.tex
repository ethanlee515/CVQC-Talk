  \note{
    \footnotesize
    Answer the earlier Challenges questions at the highlighted MBQC
    output. Good means plaintext-good: no plaintext-level modification
    beneath the prescribed pad. A ciphertext-level operation outside
    the pad can still affect the whole batch and the prover's memory.
    Up to negligible distance of the complete state, including its
    acceptance flag, the accepting branch decomposes into components
    with a bounded bad support. Most copies are ideal beneath their
    pads, and the remaining plaintext modifications factor across
    the bad copies, with one global outside operation. This is a joint,
    componentwise statement, not a claim that the physical batch is
    an independent product or that the verifier knows which copies
    are good. It gives the error structure and joint support bound
    needed for distillation. The conversion to Pauli pads preserves
    this invariant. It does not yet promise correctly authenticated
    physical codewords: the outside attack remains, which is the
    challenge on the following slide. Source: MBQC output theorem
    in Cut-and-Choose; MBQC output satisfies the distillation invariant.
  }
