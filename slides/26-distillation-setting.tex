  \note<1>{
    \footnotesize
    The paper's pTEnc is the plaintext trap encoding, after the hidden
    permutation and before the outer Pauli pad:
    $\mathsf{pTEnc}_{\pi}(\ket{\psi})
      =\pi(\mathsf{QECEnc}(\ket{\psi})\otimes
       \ket{0}^{\otimes\lambda}\otimes\ket{+}^{\otimes\lambda})$.
    Thus TEnc includes both this encoding and the Pauli pad. The same
    permutation is shared by the four blocks, while their pad keys are
    independent; in the four-copy expression, x and z concatenate the
    four physical pad strings. Each copy has $3\lambda$ physical qubits.
    The MBQC protocol first downgrades its X-T pads to these Pauli pads.
    We now examine the input to the distillation routine, before applying
    the transversal C-dist circuit. Source: Distillation section,
    plaintext trap encoding and the one-bad-input reduction.
  }
  \note<2>{
    \footnotesize
    Fix one component of the joint factorizable invariant whose bad support
    is the singleton $\{i\}$. A-pt-i acts on the ith plaintext trap-encoded
    block and as identity on the other three. It may depend on the hidden
    permutation, but not on the Pauli pad keys. The outside A-ct is also
    independent of those keys and may act globally on all four blocks and
    the prover's private workspace; an initially key-independent auxiliary
    state is implicit. The vector shown is a possibly subnormalized
    component, not an assertion that the entire physical input is a pure
    product state. The full state sums components and averages uniform
    reference pad keys, with the verifier's key register retained and the
    required aggregate normalization bounds. Components with several bad
    copies have a product of local plaintext factors instead. Focus on the
    singleton case here, then work through the circuit and the twirling
    reduction before stating the output characterization. Source:
    factorizable attack map and the Technical Overview's 4-to-1 reduction.
  }
