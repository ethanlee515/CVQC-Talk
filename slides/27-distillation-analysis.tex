  \note<1>{
    \footnotesize
    Draw the preceding input expression, choosing copy three as the
    single plaintext-bad copy. Each input is the plaintext trap encoding
    pTEnc-pi of zero, with its QEC data, traps, and hidden permutation
    already included. Each slashed wire bundles $3\lambda$ physical
    qubits. A-pt acts on copy three alone, then the outer Pauli pad
    encrypts all four copies, then A-ct may act jointly on them and on
    implicit prover memory. The three CNOTs are transversal. Copies
    two, three, and four are measured in Z, X, and Z. The final
    OTP-inverse box means the classical outcome unmasking using the
    Clifford-updated measured-copy keys; it is not a quantum operation
    after measurement. The first output remains encrypted. Trap,
    syndrome, and logical checks are implicit after unmasking.
  }
  \note<2>{
    \footnotesize
    Move the ciphertext attack to after the prescribed Clifford U,
    where U is C-dist applied at every physical coordinate. For an input
    attack alone, replace A-ct by $U A_{\mathrm{ct}} U^\dagger$ and
    keep the same label. A malicious implementation is treated in the
    same honest-unitary-before-the-attack representation: include its
    complete deviation, retained reports, and private workspace in the
    new outside operation. The circuit and this operation are independent
    of the verifier's keys and permutation. Source: Technical Overview,
    4-to-1 reduction; twirled distillation adversary.
  }
  \note<3>{
    \footnotesize
    Push the Pauli pad through the transversal Clifford. Its keys change
    bijectively: $U X^u Z^v = X^{u'}Z^{v'} U$, up to global phase.
    Use u,v here to distinguish the secret pad coordinates from the
    plaintext Pauli indices x,z introduced later. For each aligned
    coordinate, the updated X keys are
    $(u_1+u_3,u_2+u_1,u_3,u_4+u_3)$, and the Z keys are
    $(v_1+v_2,v_2,v_3+v_1+v_2+v_4,v_4)$, with sums modulo two.
    The label OTP now denotes these updated keys. Its measured-copy
    coordinates agree with the outcome-unmasking box on the right.
    This bijective change preserves independent uniform reference keys.
  }
  \note<4>{
    \footnotesize
    Move the classical outcome unmasking before the three measurements
    as inverse quantum Pauli pads on those measured registers. Only
    the outcome-flipping component is needed to implement the relabeling;
    completing it to the full inverse adds phases that cancel in the
    measured branch density operators. This gives OTP-dagger on copies
    two, three, and four. The surviving first copy is not decrypted.
    This is an analysis experiment, with the actual verifier still
    classical. Retain public reports in prover memory and keep the live
    key registers; discard only the consumed private coordinates and
    outcomes specified in the paper's equivalence lemma.
  }
  \note<5>{
    \footnotesize
    Partially twirl the measured copies. Expand the outside operation
    as $A_{\mathrm{ct}}=\sum_Q B_Q\otimes Q$, with Q a Pauli on copies
    two, three, and four, and B-Q acting on the surviving ciphertext
    and private memory. Average the consumed uniform pad coordinates.
    The measured pads and their inverses cancel, and cross terms
    between distinct Q labels vanish. The figure shows one resulting
    subnormalized Kraus component, with
    $Q=Q_2\otimes Q_3\otimes Q_4$. Its coefficient B-Q retains the
    branch weight and is not necessarily unitary. The three separate
    Q-two, Q-three, and Q-four gates show that this Pauli is a tensor
    product, not an entangling operation. The surviving first-copy
    pad remains live and is drawn explicitly before B-Q. Only the
    measured-copy pads cancel. This is equality of
    the full joint state after averaging, not a fixed-key operator
    identity. Source: Equivalence of original and twirled distillation
    adversaries.
  }
  \note<6>{
    \footnotesize
    Expand the local plaintext operator in the Pauli basis and first
    follow one term $X^xZ^z$ on copy three. Here x,z index that attack,
    not the secret one-time-pad keys. They are bit strings over the
    physical positions in one block. This is a termwise calculation;
    the plaintext attack is a coherent sum, not an independent
    classical Pauli mixture produced by this step. Its coefficients,
    which may depend on pi, must be recombined using the accepted-output
    argument later in the paper. Keep the B-Q component and all
    normalization factors carried by that component.
  }
  \note<7>{
    \footnotesize
    Propagate the third-copy Pauli through the three transversal CNOTs.
    A control X propagates to its target, while a control Z stays
    on the control. Thus $X_3^x$ becomes $X_1^xX_3^xX_4^x$ and
    $Z_3^z$ stays $Z_3^z$, so the displayed post-Clifford attacks are
    X-x on copies one and four, X-x Z-z on copy three, and identity
    on copy two. This is an operator identity on every physical
    coordinate. The Pauli Q remains after these propagated plaintext
    factors, and B-Q remains outside the live surviving pad.
    The keys and the plaintext-encoding stabilizer structure have
    not been changed by this rewriting. Stop before imposing acceptance:
    next, use the checked fourth copy to identify the surviving
    X-type component in terms of its ciphertext Pauli Q-four.
    Source: plaintext-component propagation and the one-bad-input
    reduction, i equals three.
  }
